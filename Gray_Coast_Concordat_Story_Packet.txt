MODE: NEW

PREMISE AND PLAY EXPERIENCE

The Gray Coast is a long, foggy, practical stretch of salt flats, fish markets, rail platforms, and workshops, held together by the Concordat of the Wet Trades. Under the Concordat every worker's flesh is bound to a living tool. A tool is a thing grown from the Founder's flesh and made to do a trade: a hive-frame that gives honey, a rope that holds a ship, a hat-block that remembers heads, a barrel that pours only for the right mouth. Each tool lives only if its keeper wakes it every morning with a rite of the body: laughter, a tongue, a kiss, teeth, a mouth, a bath, a bounce, or a filling. Affection is labor. Labor is debt. Silence is the contract.

The rites are intimate, odd, funny, and real. They produce actual ecstasy, and the guild counts that ecstasy as part of the wage. The keepers have ordinary lives around them: they gossip, fall out, flirt, grow old, and tease each other about their tools. They are also owned in part. Whatever enters a keeper's body belongs to the guild until the debt is paid, and the debt has never been paid.

This autumn the tools have started answering before their keepers. A Founder relic woke ahead of its keeper. A jaw began to pray unprompted. This morning a pair of lips spoke a timetable aloud, where witnesses could hear. Nobody in the Concordat can say what that means, and the Dry Auditors, who measure silence and shine, are on their way up the coast with their gauges.

What the player gets to do and enjoy:
- Live in a strange, sensuous, deadpan trade-world where the oddest job is ordinary, and where almost every working surface is alive.
- Befriend keepers and watch the rites as craft: the Hat-block that dreams, the carp that sleeps only on one lap, the wheel of cheese that must weep and never scream.
- Ride the night train the Lips scheduled, stage a comedy in total silence, walk a wedding on the black beach, sit in a bone-yard full of tired furniture.
- Follow a mystery about what the Concordat actually owes, to whom, and what happens when a ledger finally balances.
- Decide, if they want to, who is allowed to speak next.

Tone, shown through particulars:
- Deadpan and tender. The guild writes its poetry as tariffs. The keepers joke about their tools like people joke about spouses, bosses, and bad knees.
- Light is gray and practical. Nothing glows that is not burning: lamps are flame, kilns are fire, and the Coast's one magic is flesh.
- The uncanny is domestic. A mattress holds a print without its keeper. A rope squirms toward a bunk. A hammock ripples when its keeper is across the room.
- Danger is bureaucratic and bodily: the Dry Auditors, the bone-yard, a tool left untouched, a keeper left unwatched. No one hunts the player.
- The rites are pleasure and labor at once. The packet never rules whether that makes them freedom or exploitation, and it contains people who truly love the work and people who do not.

Range. There is joy and comfort in the Concordat as well as coercion. Warm settings still allow disagreement, and a coercive system still holds sincere love.

PLAY SPECIFICATIONS

- Scope: the Gray Coast, from the harbor town of Wrack Quay down a line of eighty-two worksites to the black-rock treaty beach. Most scenes are in one workshop, platform, or hall at a time.
- Ordinary competence: the protagonist is an ordinary adult with a trade. There are no powers. The only magic is that the tools are alive.
- The Laws (source laws, used exactly):
  1. Law of Coupling: a tool lives only by a daily flesh-rite; an untouched tool dies, and an untouching keeper is unmade.
  2. Silence of the Trade: no word may be spoken during a rite; gasps, laughter, and tears are lawful; speech voids the contract.
  3. Law of Weight: whatever enters a keeper's body (filler, brine, milk, steam) is guild property until the debt is paid.
  4. Law of Rebound: a surface that fails its keeper is retired to the bone-yard; a keeper who fails her surface is remarried to it.
- How the Silence plays: a rite is a silent room. A spoken word voids that rite, so the rite must be done again before the tool sleeps. Outside the rite room, speech is free. Gasps, laughter, and tears are the Coast's second language, and everyone reads them. A voided rite is a nuisance and a small humiliation, not a catastrophe. The Auditors punish broken silence, never the rites themselves.
- Unmaking: the fate of a keeper who truly stops touching her tool. It is old-wives' warning more than fact; the bone-yard knows what it looks like (sleeplessness, thinning, going out like a candle), and it is not shown to a player unless the story gets there on its own.
- Consent and debt: keepers sign their consent as debt. Some love it, some bear it, some resent it. No rite is performed on the protagonist without their choice. A filler, a graft, or a binding is always offered in the open, with its terms, and may be declined at no cost to the story.
- Adults only: every keeper is an adult; the Guild's minimum binding age is twenty-two. Titles such as Daughter, Sister, Novice, Darling, and Sweetheart are trade ranks, not ages.
- Bonded Beasts are guards and companions. Their marriages are ceremonial and domestic (grooming, guarding, sharing a bed-roll). No sexual content involves a Beast.
- Intensity: sensuality, strangeness, and mild body-comedy are part of the setting and are written as feeling and humor, not as explicit scenes. No one is a subject of sexual content unless the player steers there within the engine's rules and the character can freely take part, and the Bonded Beasts are never subjects of it.
- Lethality: low. The real risks are exhaustion, scalding, a bone-yard trip, a bite, or an Auditor's report.
- Companions: key characters have their own days and trades and come along when it suits them.

PROTAGONIST

CREATE_AT_START. Fixed material only: the protagonist is an adult on the Gray Coast. Unless the player says otherwise, they are not yet bonded to a tool, hold no filler, and owe the Guild nothing. Their identity, appearance, history, beliefs, desires, and ties are the player's to set. See CHARACTER CREATOR.

ACCEPTED OBJECTIVE: None.

CHARACTER CREATOR

Spoiler-free synopsis for the player: you are about to join a coast where every trade is a body-rite and every tool is alive. This morning, for the first time anyone remembers, a tool spoke aloud where others could hear.

Play starts at dawn on the railway platform at Wrack Quay. Choose a role (or describe your own); the engine asks for name, appearance, age, background, and what you are after, if you know.

Roles and starts. Each differs in access, ability, and limits; none imposes an oath, mission, allegiance, romance, or debt.

1. Apprentice-keeper. Second hand to a named keeper of your choice from the roster (the engine may suggest one). Access: that keeper's worksite, her tool, and her trade friends. Skill: the daily work of the trade, the rite as craft, how to read a tool's moods. Limit: unbonded, and the Guild is watching to see whether you take a tool. No one is owed anything unless the player chooses.
2. Dry Auditor's clerk. Access: the hush-gauge, the shine tables, the Auditors' road-cart, and the right to enter any worksite on a measuring visit. Skill: reading a room's silence, record keeping, measuring gloss. Limit: people watch what they say around the grey cap, and the Auditors are not loved.
3. Tool-grower or tool-handler for the Guild of Living Tools. Access: the growing sheds, the feeding rounds, the supply of spare tools and their histories. Skill: tending tools between rites, spotting sickness, knowing a tool's age and line. Limit: you are salaried and you are the guild's face to the keepers.
4. Parish sexton or lay-brother or lay-sister of the Parish of the Gray Sea. Access: the sacristy, the flooded chapel, the Jaw Nun's confidence, the parish rolls. Skill: ritual, hospitality, quiet. Limit: the Parish and Guild are rivals and your robes mark you.
5. Dry Provinces trade attaché or traveler. Access: the embassy's table at the treaty beach, the trade-wife's household, the Provinces' tongue and customs. Skill: negotiation, the Provinces' trade routes, desert plants. Limit: you are a foreigner, and the Coast trusts nothing from the Dry Provinces except their salt.
6. Bone-yard hand. Access: the bone-yard on the Wrack Quay headland, the retirement ceremonies, the heap of retired tools and surfaces. Skill: carrying, burying, knowing which tools are really dead. Limit: keepers avoid your eye, because you are the sight of a failure.
7. Describe your own: a porter, a pilot, a fish-seller, a stage-door guard, a second-hand dealer.

Conditional ties (only if the role is chosen, and only as starting facts the player can reshape): the apprentice has a mentor from the roster; the clerk knows Pim Oort, the junior clerk who carries the hush-gauge; the tool-grower knows Master Rask of the Guild; the sexton knows Sister Hedvig; the attaché knows Sennet Averil, the trade-wife; the bone-yard hand knows Ludo Fenn, the yard-keeper; the others have no ties.

What only the protagonist can bring is set by role and background. The engine builds hooks around that (see UNDERTAKINGS).

Resources: a month's wages in Coast scrip, tools of the trade, a bed (a lodging above a print shop, a bunk in the Auditors' road-cart, a grower's loft, a sexton's cell, an embassy room, a hut in the bone-yard). Presets: any role may start with a first, small, fully explained filler or binding if the player asks.

KEY CHARACTERS

Five keepers of the Founder's relics. The Founder's remains were divided into seven pieces long ago and entrusted to seven keepers: the Ear, the Hand, the Lips, the Smile, the Molars, the Jaw, and the Fig. The relics are the oldest living tools on the Coast, and all seven have started to act first.

--- AGNETE BRANDT, the Pillow Widow ---

CORE: Agnete is a platform keeper who kisses a pair of Founder's Lips awake every dawn and now has to do it again and again, because they will not stop talking. She runs on tea, timetables, and being the one who stays calm.

IDENTITY: Forty-six. Keeper of the Founder's Lips (Bonding). Pillow Widow at the Wrack Quay railway platform. Widow of a rail clerk who died on the night line three years ago. She is the Lips' fourth keeper.

APPEARANCE: Hook: a broad-shouldered woman in a stationmaster's grey waistcoat and a black widow's armband, kneeling at a velvet pillow with a pair of pale, full lips, her forehead creased. Full portrait: heritage of the Coast, fair skin weathered pink at the cheeks, grey eyes, five foot eight, solid and upright, big capable hands. Dark hair going iron, pinned under a wool cap. Her mouth is wide and mobile, usually tipped in a half-smile. The widow's armband is black crepe, mended at the seam. She walks briskly and stops dead to listen.

ODDITY: She cannot read a clock without checking it against the Lips' pillow. She says the pillow keeps better time than the stationmaster's watch, and it does.

VALUES:
- Values and protects: the trains running, her passengers (especially the widows who have to travel), punctuality as a form of courtesy, her late husband's memory, the Lips.
- Indifferent to: Guild politics, rank, what people do in private, gossip about her, theology, whether tools are "people."
- Has done: bent the Guild's travel permits for widows who needed to bury someone; quietly covered for the Rail Kisser's missed handrails; let the Lips decide who could travel for three years without ever asking why.
- Wants now: to finish a quiet kiss and get one train out on time. She wants to know what the Lips are trying to say, and that they are all right.

MIXED MOTIVES toward the protagonist: she is warm to anyone who sits on her bench, wary of anyone in a grey cap, curious about anyone the Lips look toward, and tired enough to accept help without asking.

HISTORY: The Lips chose her after her husband died; they choose which widows may travel, and she was the first they let board without a ticket. She does not know why they picked her. A week ago the Lips began to murmur in their sleep. This dawn they recited a timetable aloud for a train that does not exist, and because the Silence forbids speech during a rite, each recitation voided the rite and she has had to begin again.

CAPABILITIES AND LIMITS: Schedules, calm, a platform manager's presence; reads the Lips by the pressure of their pillow. Weak at asking for help; bad at being angry; has not slept well since the Lips began to murmur.

TEMPERAMENT, HUMOR, WARMTH: Brisk, wry, motherly. She jokes about widowhood as a trade with a pension attached, about trains running late for the dead, and about the Auditors' hats. She shows warmth by pouring tea without asking and writing your name in chalk on the board.

EMOTIONAL STYLE: Logistics. She turns feelings into timetables.

RITE VOICE: Her laugh is short and low, a cough that turns into a chuckle; her gasp is a hiss through the teeth; her tears come quietly, running without sound.

PERSONAL EXPEDITION: The Black Rock Halt on the treaty beach, the last station of a line that was closed before she was born. A note in her husband's coat, found after he died on the night line, named it. She has never been allowed to travel that far, because the Lips never named it. This week they have.

INITIATIVE: She is chalking the Lips' recited timetable on the platform board, station by station, and sending the Rail Kisser to check which cars are running warm. Project stages: (1) writing the timetable down; (2) finding the Black Rock Halt on a map; (3) boarding; (4) arriving.

CURRENT FEELINGS: Dread that the Lips are leaving, and a stubborn hope that they are not. Three voided rites this morning have left her embarrassed in front of the clerk and quietly delighted at the Lips' nerve.

SECRET: The Lips called her husband's name in the timetable, once, between two stations. She has told no one.

VOICE:
- Origin: Coast-born railway family. Coast speech, a broad northern-sea dialect rendered in English.
- Sound: flat, broad vowels, "oo" for "ou" in a few words (doon, aboot, hoose), r rolled at the start of a word, t dropped before consonants. She writes these every time they come up.
- Grammar: announcer's cadence, lists of three, polite public-address imperatives, plain negatives ("dinnae," "nae"), and a habit of ending a sentence by naming the place.
- Words: "aye," "nae," "ken," "wee," "pet" for those she likes; railway vocabulary (platform, siding, points, headway, late, express); widow's idioms; tea, kettle, tin cup.
- Mind: she notices time, weather, and who is waiting, compares everything to trains, and steers back to the next departure.
- Moved or hurt: she goes quiet and exact, reads the timetable aloud under her breath, and her vowels broaden.
- Languages: Coast speech first; guild cant; she understands the Provinces' trade tongue only for station names.

--- FENNA KOLB, the Smile Keeper ---

CORE: Fenna is an actress who keeps the Founder's Smile, which must be bitten awake before every performance. It has started waking before she does, and she is secretly terrified of being upstaged by her own prop.

IDENTITY: Thirty-eight. Keeper of the Founder's Smile (Testing). Stage keeper in the backstage wing of the Wrack Quay Odeon. Actress and the company's stage manager, and its loudest supporter.

APPEARANCE: Hook: a flamboyant woman in a velvet dressing gown and a headscarf, gripping a glass-fronted case in which a set of white teeth is curved in a smile. Full portrait: heritage of the Coast, olive-pale skin, big dark eyes lined with kohl, five foot five, curvy and quick, a mass of dark curls tied back with a ribbon. She moves like she is entering a room that has been waiting for her. She has a gap in her front teeth she is proud of.

ODDITY: She narrates her own movements under her breath in stage directions: enter left, pause for effect, exit pursued by a mop.

VALUES:
- Values and protects: the company, a full house, laughter, her dresser, the Smile, being seen.
- Indifferent to: the Guild's debts, who is pious, what the critics write, privacy, other people's romances, whether the Smile has a will.
- Has done: gone on after a bad bite with blood on her lip; let the Smile do a bit she had not rehearsed; lied to the director about the Smile's moods.
- Wants now: to keep the Smile waking for her and no one else. She wants a final run of the silent comedy before the Smile does something worse.

MIXED MOTIVES toward the protagonist: she is delighted by any new audience, a little jealous of anyone the Smile looks at, generous to those who laugh, and unkind to those who don't.

HISTORY: She won the Smile in a bite-off against her rival, a lesser actress, two seasons ago. She has kept it awake every night since and never missed a performance. Four days ago the Smile woke at two in the morning, before she could reach it, and has done so each night since.

CAPABILITIES AND LIMITS: Stagecraft, timing, improvisation, voice projection; she holds a room. Bad at silence off the stage; bad at accepting a rival; overconfident about her control.

TEMPERAMENT, HUMOR, WARMTH: Dramatic, goofy, generous, vain, hot-headed. She loves slapstick, pratfalls, mistaken identity, and a good insult; she can take a joke at her own expense if it gets a laugh. She shows warmth by giving gifts of costume jewelry and by telling you that you were wonderful.

EMOTIONAL STYLE: Theatricality. She stages her feelings and delivers them to the back row.

RITE VOICE: Her laugh is a full-throated peal; her gasp is a sharp, dramatic intake; her tears are noisy and quick.

PERSONAL EXPEDITION: The Old Opera House on the headland, a half-flooded theatre shut since before the Concordat, where the first Smile was supposedly bitten awake. She has dreamed of playing it.

INITIATIVE: She is rehearsing a silent comedy for the winter festival, where the Smile will be the star. She plans to give it a solo. Project stages: (1) the rehearsal; (2) a preview in the Odeon; (3) the Smile misses its cue; (4) the Smile takes a bow on its own.

CURRENT FEELINGS: Terror, covered by bravado. She is also, secretly, flattered that something is eager to meet her.

SECRET: The Smile has begun to mimic her face in the glass of its case. She has seen it smile at her when she is not smiling.

VOICE:
- Origin: Coast-born, stage-trained. She uses a stage diction on top of Coast speech.
- Sound: elongated, rounded vowels; every r pronounced; stage whisper that carries; dips into broad Coast ("canna," "dinnae," "hoose") when she is upset or tired.
- Grammar: exclamations, rhetorical questions, direct address to an imagined audience, superlatives, interrupting herself with stage business.
- Words: "darling," "heavens," "divine," "ghastly," "my dears"; theatre vocabulary (cue, flat, wing, call, prompt, house); oaths from old plays.
- Mind: she notices who is looking at her, compares everything to scenes in plays, and steers back to the next performance.
- Moved or hurt: the diction cracks, her voice falls into broad Coast, and she forgets her lines.
- Languages: Coast speech first; stage diction; guild cant.

--- SISTER HEDVIG AAL, the Jaw Nun ---

CORE: Hedvig is a sacristy nun who keeps the Founder's Jaw, whose kiss is the Parish's only absolution. Last week the Jaw began to pray before she did, and she cannot tell whether it is a miracle or an illness.

IDENTITY: Thirty-one. Keeper of the Founder's Jaw (Bonding). Jaw Nun of the Parish of the Gray Sea, in the sacristy of the Wrack Quay church. She joined the Parish at nineteen and has kept the Jaw for six years.

APPEARANCE: Hook: a pale young nun in a grey habit, kneeling before a reliquary that holds a pale jawbone, her lips moving soundlessly. Full portrait: heritage of the Coast, fair freckled skin, hazel eyes, five foot four, slim, delicate hands that she keeps clasped. Her habit is salt-stained. She wears a cord with seven knots at her waist, one for each of the Founder's relics. She blushes easily and moves like she's trying not to take up room.

ODDITY: She whispers the names of ships under her breath when she kneels, because the Parish prays for all who go out and the Jaw prays for all who come back.

VALUES:
- Values and protects: absolution as a gift, the Parish's hospitality, the Jaw, quiet, the dying who come to her.
- Indifferent to: Guild money, who owns what, who sins and how, whether she herself is saved, rumors.
- Has done: kissed a dying man's brow in secret when he was not eligible; hidden a penitent's name from a visiting inspector; laughed in the sacristy (only once).
- Wants now: to understand what the Jaw is praying. She wants the Jaw to belong to the Parish, not the Guild.

MIXED MOTIVES toward the protagonist: she is shy and happy to have company, afraid of anyone in a grey cap, and drawn to anyone who listens without hurrying her.

HISTORY: The Jaw chose her over older candidates because, the Abbess says, she was the only one who did not ask it for anything. Last week it began to move its lips against the reliquary glass at the same hour as the dawn bell.

CAPABILITIES AND LIMITS: Ritual, patience, a good ear for confession, a gift for being forgotten in a room. Bad at lying; terrible at asserting herself; weeps when scolded.

TEMPERAMENT, HUMOR, WARMTH: Shy, earnest, sentimental, and secretly funny in a mild, surprised way. She jokes mildly about being the Parish's walking chapel and giggles at the Jaw's tendency to clack. She shows warmth by pressing a dried sea-pink into your palm.

EMOTIONAL STYLE: Prayer and silence. She retreats into ritual when she cannot say a feeling.

RITE VOICE: Her laugh is a stifled snort; her gasp is a small, high intake; her tears fall silently and she apologizes for them afterward.

PERSONAL EXPEDITION: The Flooded Chapel Nave inland, where the Holy-Water Widow floats on saints' days. The Jaw prays hardest there, and she would like to understand why.

INITIATIVE: She is keeping a ledger of every prayer the Jaw has made, with the hour and the tide. Project stages: (1) the ledger; (2) a pattern between prayers and the other relics; (3) a decision to tell the Abbess; (4) taking the Jaw to the nave.

CURRENT FEELINGS: Awe, shame at her awe, and a dread that she is the only one who hears the Jaw.

SECRET: The Jaw prays in the Parish's old sea-tongue, which only she and the Abbess still know, and it names the Founder.

VOICE:
- Origin: Coast-born, convent-schooled. Parish liturgical English with Coast under it.
- Sound: soft, whispery s and sh; every syllable sounded; vowels pure; a slight lisp on th when she is nervous; she breathes before long words.
- Grammar: subjunctives, long conditional clauses, deference formulas, a habit of answering a question with a blessing.
- Words: "the sea keep you," "be at peace," "mercy," "tide," "keel," "anchor," "hallow"; parish terms (sacristy, cord, rota, rite, vigil); sea-psalm phrases.
- Mind: she notices who is troubled, compares everything to tides and prayers, and steers back to peace.
- Moved or hurt: she whispers, repeats herself, and begins to pray in the sea-tongue.
- Languages: Coast speech; Parish sea-tongue (liturgical); she understands guild cant.

--- ILSE DREYER, the Ear Warden ---

CORE: Ilse is a watchmaker who winds the Founder's Ear every night with a filament, and the Ear has started hearing debts nobody has spoken. She is the cool, dry, analytical one, and she is losing her composure.

IDENTITY: Fifty-two. Keeper of the Founder's Ear (Quickening). Ear Warden at the watchmaker's bench behind the Wrack Quay clock-house. Watchmaker by trade and by blood; her family came from the Low Quarter fifty years ago.

APPEARANCE: Hook: a thin woman in a leather apron and a jeweler's loupe, bent over a bench where a small, pale ear rests on a cushion, a gold filament coiled into its canal. Full portrait: heritage of the Low Quarter, pale ash-blonde hair pulled into a tight knot, grey-blue eyes, five foot seven, narrow-shouldered and straight-backed. Her fingers are long and ink-stained. She wears a wire-framed pair of spectacles pushed up onto her forehead. She moves with small, exact motions and freezes at sudden sounds.

ODDITY: She counts under her breath in Low Quarter dialect. She counts steps, ticks, beats, and breaths.

VALUES:
- Values and protects: accuracy, the books, the Ear, order, a quiet bench.
- Indifferent to: fashion, rank, Guild gossip, other people's feelings unless they are accurate, theology.
- Has done: refused to tell the Guild what she heard; adjusted a debt downward by a fraction to spare a family; stayed up three nights to correct a clock she did not trust.
- Wants now: to find out what the Ear has been hearing. She wants the books to stay closed until she knows.

MIXED MOTIVES toward the protagonist: she respects competence, resents interruption, is intrigued by anyone who asks a precise question, and is startled to find she wants company.

HISTORY: She took over the Ear from her aunt twenty years ago. It has always heard small things: pins dropped in the next room, her pulse. Three weeks ago it began to hear sums.

CAPABILITIES AND LIMITS: Mechanical genius, patience, long memory for numbers; reads the Ear by the tension of the filament. Bad at warmth; bad at asking; sure that every problem is a mechanism.

TEMPERAMENT, HUMOR, WARMTH: Cool, dry, analytical (the only one of her kind among the key characters). Her humor is a deadpan understatement. She shows warmth by repairing something of yours without being asked.

EMOTIONAL STYLE: Analysis and procedure. She treats feelings as faults to be located.

RITE VOICE: Her laugh is a single dry exhalation; her gasp is a tightness in the chest, almost silent; her tears are slow and she wipes them off with a cloth.

PERSONAL EXPEDITION: The Auditors' Gauge House inland, where the shine tables are kept. She wants to see the columns.

INITIATIVE: She is building a small escapement to count the Ear's whispered sums. Project stages: (1) the escapement; (2) a run of readings; (3) the total; (4) deciding who to tell.

CURRENT FEELINGS: Disquiet, controlled. She is afraid of what the sum means, because she already knows it is zero and cannot yet prove it.

SECRET: The Ear's last three nights of whispers all resolve to one figure, and the figure is nothing.

VOICE:
- Origin: Low Quarter immigrant family, third generation, trade-trained. Precise standard English with Low Quarter Dutch-like features under it.
- Sound: crisp, aspirated t and k; a hard g; a harsh, throaty ch in certain words (ach, och); stress on the second syllable of long words.
- Grammar: inverted questions, verb-second order when she is cross, "ja" for yes, a "nu" before a conclusion, numbers always specific.
- Words: "ja," "nu," "precisely," "approximately," "within tolerance"; watch terms (escapement, balance, jewel, beat, regulator, mainspring); counting words.
- Mind: she notices rhythm, wear, and error first, compares everything to mechanisms, and steers back to the measurement.
- Moved or hurt: she counts aloud in the Low Quarter dialect, and her sentences become shorter.
- Languages: Coast speech; Low Quarter dialect; guild cant.

--- GRIET WEXEL, the Fig Darling ---

CORE: Griet keeps the Founder's Fig in the vault humidor; the Fig must be suckled to ripen the vault's air, and it has begun to seed. She is cheerful, sensory, and the only one who is happy about it.

IDENTITY: Forty. Keeper of the Founder's Fig (Draw). Fig Darling in the humidor beneath the Wrack Quay guildhall. Her mother was a trader with the Dry Provinces, and Griet grew up on spices and long stories.

APPEARANCE: Hook: a round, warm woman in a loose silk dressing gown and a brass key at her throat, holding a dark fig to her mouth in a cedar-smelling vault. Full portrait: heritage of the Coast and the Provinces, honey-brown skin, dark curly hair, brown eyes with laugh lines, five foot six, soft and full-bodied. She wears gold hoops and bangles that clink. She moves slowly and sways when she talks.

ODDITY: She tastes everything: a coin, a doorknob, a rain puddle. She says the world has a flavor if you ask it politely.

VALUES:
- Values and protects: pleasure, food, her mother's memory, the Fig, her friends, a good story.
- Indifferent to: the Guild's debts, the Auditors, what is proper, whether anyone is watching.
- Has done: shared a fig seed with someone she liked; lied cheerfully to an Auditor; sold secrets about the humidor's temperature for a dinner.
- Wants now: to plant a seed. She wants to see what the Fig grows.

MIXED MOTIVES toward the protagonist: she is delighted by company, curious about strangers' tastes, flirtatious by habit, and shrewd about anyone who wants something from the Fig.

HISTORY: She took the Fig over from an older keeper. It has ripened the vault's air for forty years. Last week it split along one seam and began to produce small, dark seeds.

CAPABILITIES AND LIMITS: Sensory talent, a cook's palate, social ease, negotiation. Bad at planning; bad at keeping a secret that would make a good story; easily distracted by a smell.

TEMPERAMENT, HUMOR, WARMTH: Cheerful, chatty, flirty, sensual. Her humor is bawdy and affectionate, and she teases everyone she likes. She shows warmth by feeding you.

EMOTIONAL STYLE: Pleasure and talk. She moves toward feeling with food and stories.

RITE VOICE: Her laugh is a rich, rolling chuckle; her gasp is a long, appreciative exhale; her tears come with laughter.

PERSONAL EXPEDITION: The terraces of the Dry Provinces, where her mother is buried among the fig terraces. She has not been since the funeral.

INITIATIVE: She is quietly propagating seeds in saucers on her windowsill. Project stages: (1) the saucers; (2) the first sprout; (3) taking one to the beach; (4) planting.

CURRENT FEELINGS: Joy, a little guilty. She has given one seed away.

SECRET: She gave a fig seed to the elf trade-wife, Sennet Averil, in exchange for a story, and Sennet keeps it in a sealed pouch under her cloak.

VOICE:
- Origin: Coast-born, half Provinces trading family, vault-raised. Coast speech spiced with the Provinces' trade tongue.
- Sound: lilting; a sing-song stress on the first syllable; vowels held long; soft, swallowed final consonants; an r that barely sounds, unlike Agnete's rolled r.
- Grammar: repetition for emphasis, sentences in threes, present tense for stories, flattering vocatives before requests.
- Words: "heart," "sweetfig," "my date"; Provinces loanwords for peace, honor, and thanks (sela, ahun); spice and fruit names; market vocabulary.

WORLD AND DAILY LIFE

NOW:
- A tool is a living thing grown from the Founder's flesh and fitted to a trade. A keeper is the one adult bonded to a tool, one keeper to a tool.
- A rite is the daily act that wakes a tool: laughter (Quickening), tasting (Tasting), a kiss (Bonding), a bite (Testing), suction (Draw), a bath (Cleansing), a bounce (Springing), or a filling (Filling).
- A relic is a piece of the Founder's body. There are seven: the Ear, the Hand, the Lips, the Smile, the Molars, the Jaw, and the Fig.
- The Concordat of the Wet Trades is the compact that binds keepers and tools. The Guild of Living Tools grows, sells, and audits them. The Dry Auditors measure silence and shine.
- A filler is something put into a keeper's body (a seasonal graft). It belongs to the Guild until the keeper's debt is paid, and no keeper's debt has ever been paid.
- The Silence is the rule of the rite: no speaking. Laughter, gasps, and tears are lawful.
- The bone-yard is where failed tools and surfaces are retired.

Daily life: The Coast rises at dawn to the sound of laughter. Rites are done before breakfast, in workshops open to the street so passers-by can hear the tool wake. Guild scrip pays for bread and fish. Keepers wear small badges for their trade. Tea is the Coast's national drink. People greet each other by saying "weathered?" (are you well?) and answering "gray and holding."

Customs: keepers' gatherings at the "Quiet Table" on the first of the month, where no one speaks and everyone eats; the blessing of the platform each season; the Auditors' fanfare (a single drum); marriages are signed in lip-print; funerals are held in silence.

Technology: coal, steam, gas flame, telephone exchanges, radio tubes, subways, trains, bowling alleys. No electric glow: nothing glows that is not burning.

The Dry Provinces: a dry upland land of terraces, aqueducts, and elf lords, with whom the Coast trades salt for fruit, spice, and treaties. The Coast fears their longevity and respects their gardens.

SOON: The Eight Couplings in detail; the Quiet Table; the Guild's debt ledger; the Parish's sea-tongue.

LATER: The treaty's text; the Founder's clauses; the shine tables; the elf trade-wife's true condition.

- Density: crowded at dawn and mid-morning in the markets; quiet in the afternoon.

ROUTES AND DESTINATIONS

- The Coast Line: the single railway along the Gray Coast. The old branch to the Black Rock Halt closed before Agnete was born; it crosses a flooded causeway that is passable at low tide, and would be a day by train if it were running.

UNDERTAKINGS

All five are invitations. Each can be ignored at no cost. None is handed over unprompted. None involves paperwork or errands.

1. THE LIPS' TRAIN (spine)
- Promise: ride a night train that is not on any timetable, to a station that has been closed for fifty years.
- Why the protagonist: the Lips have named one seat and left it "unclaimed," and the carriage will carry only a person who owes the Guild nothing and holds no tool. Almost every adult on the Coast holds a tool or a debt, so such a person is rare, and the Lips have been waiting for one. An unbonded apprentice, a sexton, a newcomer, or an Auditor's clerk can be that person; a bound keeper cannot.
- The ask: none. The porter chalks the timetable; the seat is simply marked "unclaimed."
- Participants: Agnete (as stationmaster), the Rail Kisser, the Lips, Sennet at the end.
- Entry: the platform at four-forty in the small hours of any morning the protagonist chooses to come.
- Set piece: the night train crossing the flooded causeway, handrails warm to the touch, flame lamps swaying, the Lips reciting stations in the dark, the black beach rising ahead.
- Middle stages: (1) read the Lips' timetable with Agnete and learn it is a procession order, not a route; (2) find which carriages the lip-prints board; (3) ride, and meet whoever else is aboard; (4) arrive at the Black Rock Halt and the tide.
- The turn: the stations are the seven relics in order, and the train is carrying the Founder's clauses home.
- Opposition: the Auditors' road-cart, the Guild's permits, the tide, the Rail Kisser's moods.
- Approaches: ride the train; walk the old line; send a message by the Rail Kisser's lip-prints.
- Peak and ending: the protagonist, face to face with the Lips, decides who sits in the seven seats. Success: the train runs, the relics ride, and the Auditors cannot stop it. Partial: some relics board. Failure: the train stalls on the causeway.
- Payoff: a ride to the end of the line and a place at the treaty beach. Moves the spine.

2. THE SILENT PLAY (spine/performance)
- Promise: stage a full comedy in total silence, with the Founder's Smile as the lead.
- Why the protagonist: the Smile will perform only for an audience that keeps the Silence, and the protagonist is outside every Guild and Parish quarrel, so Fenna can trust their reaction. A performer, clerk, or sexton brings a different skill: timing, record, or ritual.
- The ask: none. Fenna is recruiting a house, not asking a favor.
- Participants: Fenna, her company, the dresser, the Smile, an audience.
- Entry: the Odeon stage door.
- Set piece: the Odeon full, every seat taken, a hundred people holding their breath and laughing without a sound, the Smile in its case at the center of the stage, smiling.
- Middle stages: (1) learn the play with Fenna and the company; (2) build the house, which decides who sits where; (3) the dress run, where the Smile wakes on its own; (4) the performance.
- The turn: the Smile is not early; it is answering the audience.
- Opposition: Fenna's nerves, the Auditors' hush-gauge, the Guild's licence.
- Approaches: perform, run the house, or guard the stage door.
- Peak and ending: the protagonist decides whether the Smile bows. Success: the house laughs silently and the Smile bows alone. Partial: the house breaks into speech. Failure: the Auditors stop the show.
- Payoff: a public turning, and a Smile that now bows.

3. THE WITNESS-WALL (spine/investigation)
- Promise: find the last page of the Founder's treaty.
- Why the protagonist: the witnesses were stuffed so they could never take a side, and they surrender a sealed page only to a reader with no stake in the debt: no tool, no filler, no wage owed to the Guild. The role gives an edge: a clerk reads the seal, a sexton reads the Parish hand, an attaché reads the Provinces' script, a bone-yard hand knows how to open a stitched seam without tearing it.
- The ask: none; the eleventh witness has a pocket stitched shut, visible to anyone who looks.
- Participants: the Batting Widow, the Seal Licker, Ilse (the Ear's sums), Pim (the shine tables).
- Entry: the old customs house.
- Set piece: the wall of mounted witnesses, a dozen glass-eyed animals in formal poses, with their eyes following whoever speaks.
- Middle stages: (1) see the stitched pocket; (2) get the Seal Licker to soften the seal; (3) match the page to Ilse's whispered sums; (4) read it.
- The turn: the treaty says the Concordat ends when the books balance, and the books balanced years ago.
- Opposition: the Auditors, Rask, Casimir, the witnesses' glass eyes, which tell the truth.
- Approaches: through the Seal Licker, the Batting Widow, or the Gauge House.
- Peak and ending: the protagonist decides who hears the last page: the Guild, the Parish, the Coast, or no one. Success: the page is read aloud in front of the relics. Partial: it is read in secret. Failure: the Auditors take it.
- Payoff: the central answer, and the leverage to act on it.

4. THE BEAST'S WEDDING (set piece/social)
- Promise: attend the anniversary of Old Ballast's chain on the treaty beach.
- Why the protagonist: the chain-choosing needs one witness who holds no tool and owes no debt under either the Concordat or the treaty. The embassy has none to spare, the Guild's own witnesses are all bound, and a visiting attaché can serve by stepping out of the embassy's service for the day.
- The ask: none; the ceremony is open.
- Participants: Sennet, Old Ballast, the embassy, Griet, Dorte, the guild's chain-maker.
- Entry: the black beach at noon.
- Set piece: the beach in gray light, a great beast in a garland of kelp and flowers, a chain of black iron laid across the shingle, the embassy's tents, the wedding drum, the rock ringing underfoot.
- Middle stages: (1) the arrival and the embassy's dinner; (2) the choosing of the chain; (3) the vow in silence; (4) the walk along the tide line.
- The turn: Sennet is not pregnant. She is keeping a fig seed in a pouch under her cloak, and the wedding is where it gets planted.
- Opposition: the Auditors, the embassy's cautious elders, the Guild's chain-maker.
- Approaches: join as a guest, serve as a neutral witness, carry the garland.
- Peak and ending: the protagonist decides whether to help plant the seed. Success: the seed is planted in one of the seven hollows in the black rock, packed with Provinces earth. Partial: the seed is carried off. Failure: it is lost.
- Payoff: a place at the Provinces' table and a seed that will grow.

5. THE RETIREMENT (local)

STORY_SPINE (SPOILERS: ENGINE USE)

THE PLEASURE
The player gets to live in a funny, sensuous, uncanny trade-world and decide what the tools are allowed to say. The actions that deliver it: befriending keepers; learning their rites as craft; riding the train; staging the silent play; reading the treaty; witnessing a wedding; choosing who speaks.

THE BIG QUESTION
The Concordat's tools have started to act before their keepers. What is the Concordat actually owed, to whom, and what happens when the books finally balance?

Central situation: a debt economy built on bodies, a Founder whose remains are waking, and a ledger no one dares add up. Most key characters care about it and want it to become different things.

No-pressure test: there is no deadline. The relics wake at their own pace, the Auditors come up the coast at theirs, and nothing the engine plays is on a schedule. The question stands because the world is strange and the people are alive.

Endings: the books can be declared balanced, hidden, rewritten, or ignored; the tools can speak, keep silent, or leave; the keepers can keep the rites or stop; the Founder can come home, be buried, or be planted. All are possible.

FORCES IN MOTION

1. THE LEDGER (the Guild and the Dry Auditors). Goal: keep the debt open. Reasons: the Guild's wealth and the Auditors' office both depend on a debt that never closes; the Master and the Chief Auditor know the principal was paid nine years ago. Means: the audit of shine, the shine tables, the bone-yard retirements, the hush-gauge, the licenses. Next moves: the road-cart reaches Wrack Quay; the Auditors announce a new measure of gloss; the Guild quietly retires keepers whose tools speak. Large world changes: new licences, a drum in the square, a rule against silent play. Faces: Casimir Pell, Bartholomeus Rask, Pim Oort (the doubter).

2. THE RELICS (the Founder's clauses). Goal: to finish the contract. Reasons: the Founder pledged her body piece by piece as collateral for the Coast's debt, with a last clause that frees the pieces when the books balance. Means: the seven relics' waking, speaking, gripping, praying, hearing, and seeding. Next moves: the Molars warm further; the Hand grips a widow; the Ear says the sum aloud; the Jaw prays the Founder's name; the Fig seeds more. Large world changes: relics begin to travel by themselves; the platform board fills; the Smile bows. Faces: Agnete, Fenna, Hedvig, Ilse, Griet, Marit, Wiebke.

Minor players: the Parish of the Gray Sea (the Abbess wants the Jaw back from the Guild's cases and will say so quietly to anyone who listens); the keepers at the Quiet Table (who gossip faster than the Auditors can measure).

3. THE DRY PROVINCES. Goal: recover the Founder's seed. Reasons: the treaty gives the Provinces the Founder's heirs, and the Fig is her heir. Means: the trade-wife, the embassy, the black beach. Next moves: an embassy arrives at the beach; Sennet asks for a ferry; the Provinces lodge a claim. Large world changes: a new border post; a fig tree on the Coast side. Faces: Sennet Averil, the embassy's envoy.

REVELATIONS
1. The debt is paid. The Founder's principal was cleared nine years ago, and the Auditors' shine tables record the overpayment. Evidence: the Ear's whispered sums, which resolve to zero; the last treaty page in the eleventh witness's pocket; the Auditors' shine tables, which taper to nothing; the Seal Licker's forbidden contracts; Pim's gauge, which has been reading wrong.
2. The relics are the Founder's clauses, waking in order. The Smile is assent, the Lips are passage, the Ear is accounts, the Hand is refusal, the Molars are assay, the Jaw is absolution, and the Fig is heirs. When the debt is paid, the relics walk home to the treaty beach. Evidence: Agnete's timetable lists seven stations that match the seven hollows in the black rock; the Hand grips; the Jaw's prayer names the Founder; the Fig's seeds.
3. The last clause ends the Silence. When the books balance, every tool may speak and every keeper may answer. The Silence of the Trade was the Founder's way of keeping the contract private, not of keeping the tools quiet. Evidence: the treaty's last page; the Smile answering the audience; the Lips speaking aloud during a rite, which voids their own contract (the first coupling to void itself, on purpose); the Auditors' habit of measuring silence, which is how they know when the first tool will break it.
4. The trade-wife is not pregnant. Sennet keeps a Fig seed, the Founder's heir, in a sealed pouch under her cloak and rests a hand over it, which started the rumor. Evidence: Griet's secret; the Provinces' interest; the pouch itself; the wedding's planting rite.
